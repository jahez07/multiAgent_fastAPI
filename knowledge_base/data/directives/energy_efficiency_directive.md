# EU Energy Efficiency Directive (2012/27/EU, amended 2023/1791)

## Article 3 — Energy efficiency targets
Each Member State shall set an indicative national energy efficiency target, based on either primary or final energy consumption, primary or final energy savings, or energy intensity. Member States shall notify those targets to the Commission. The Union's target is to reduce energy consumption by at least 11.7% by 2030 compared to the 2020 reference scenario projections.

## Article 4 — Building renovation
Member States shall establish a long-term strategy for mobilising investment in the renovation of the national stock of residential and commercial buildings, both public and private. This strategy shall encompass cost-effective approaches to renovations relevant to the building type and climatic zone, policies and measures to stimulate cost-effective deep renovations, and policies and measures to target the worst-performing segments of the national building stock.

## Article 5 — Exemplary role of public bodies' buildings
Each Member State shall ensure that at least 3% of the total floor area of heated or cooled buildings that is owned and occupied by its central government is renovated each year to meet at least the minimum energy performance requirements. Member States may introduce alternative approaches to achieve equivalent energy savings in public buildings, including deep renovations and measures that change occupant behaviour.

## Article 7 — Energy savings obligation
Each Member State shall achieve cumulative end-use energy savings at least equivalent to new savings each year from 1 January 2014 to 31 December 2020 of 1.5% of the annual energy sales to final customers. This may be achieved through an energy efficiency obligation scheme or alternative policy measures including energy or CO2 taxes, financing schemes, regulations or voluntary agreements, standards and norms, energy labelling schemes, and training and education.

## Article 8 — Energy audits and management systems
Member States shall promote the availability to all final customers of high-quality energy audits which are cost-effective. Member States shall ensure that enterprises that are not SMEs carry out an energy audit at the latest by 5 December 2015 and at least every four years from the date of the previous energy audit. These audits shall be carried out in an independent and cost-effective manner by qualified or accredited experts.

## Article 14 — Promotion of efficiency in heating and cooling
Member States shall carry out a comprehensive assessment of the potential for the application of high-efficiency cogeneration and efficient district heating and cooling. The comprehensive assessment shall take full account of useful and economically justified potential assessed by means of a cost-benefit analysis covering the territory.

## Article 15 — Energy transformation, transmission, and distribution
Member States shall ensure that an assessment is undertaken of the energy efficiency potentials of their gas and electricity infrastructure. Member States shall adopt concrete measures and investments for the introduction of cost-effective energy efficiency improvements in the network infrastructure, with a timetable for their introduction.
